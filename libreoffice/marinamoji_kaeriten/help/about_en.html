marinaMoji 01 / 08

Japanese writing, from input to typography.

marinaMoji brings specialized Japanese typography to LibreOffice Writer— from ruby readings and inline annotations to vertical composition and character-cell fitting.

The extension can be used on its own. It is also designed to complement the marinaMoji input method, which provides Japanese text entry and tools for working with historical character forms.

Together they support writing that attends to the particular demands of Japanese texts, including scholarly and historical materials.

Japanese typography

Ruby · ルビ

Words beside words

Ruby allows a reading or annotation to accompany Japanese characters without replacing the original text. In vertical writing the reading traditionally sits beside the base characters; in horizontal writing it sits above them.

Editors use ruby for pronunciation, unfamiliar readings, and cases where the written character and its interpretation must both remain visible. The base text stays editable and searchable as ordinary text.

How to use

Select the base characters, choose Apply ruby, and enter the reading in the dialog. Remove ruby clears the reading from the selection or from a caret inside the ruby span. The result is native Writer ruby formatting.

Japanese typography

Warichū · 割注

A note within the text

Warichū gives a text a second, quieter voice. A brief annotation appears as two smaller lines within the main line of Japanese writing, allowing an explanation to interrupt the passage without leaving it.

Unlike a footnote, the remark remains exactly where the reader encounters the words it explains. Warichū is particularly useful for short clarifications, dates, bibliographical details, and editorial observations, and has a long place in Japanese scholarly and literary typography.

How to use

Select the annotation text, rather than the entire paragraph, and choose Apply warichū. To restore ordinary text, select the annotation or place the caret inside it and choose Remove warichū. Writer arranges the annotation as two smaller lines within the surrounding text.

Japanese typography

Emphasis · 圏点・傍点

The art of drawing attention

In Japanese typography, emphasis need not change the shape or weight of a character. Instead, a small mark placed alongside it can draw the reader’s attention while leaving the character itself untouched.

These marks, known as kenten or bōten, may take the form of sesame-shaped marks, dots, or circles. Particularly at home in vertical writing, they offer a distinctive alternative to boldface or italics, allowing emphasis to be expressed through the space around the text.

How to use

Select the characters to emphasize and choose Apply emphasis marks to use the document’s default style. Choose Remove emphasis to clear marks from the selected text.

For a particular mark—such as black sesame, a filled disc, an open circle, or a dot—use the Emphasis marks menu. The menu also lets you change the document default without rewriting passages already marked.

Tate-chū-yoko · 縦中横

A little horizontal space

Vertical Japanese sometimes contains short sequences that read more naturally as a horizontal unit. Digits are the familiar case. Tate-chū-yoko keeps such a run horizontal inside an otherwise vertical line, without changing the surrounding writing direction.

The technique preserves the rhythm of the column while accommodating compact material that would feel awkward if stacked character by character.

How to use

In a vertical paragraph, select a short Latin or digit run (two characters is the usual case; three or four may ask for confirmation; longer or mixed Japanese selections are refused). Choose Apply tate-chū-yoko. Remove tate-chū-yoko removes the horizontal combination, returning the characters to the paragraph’s normal writing direction.

Japanese typography

Jidori · 字取り

Making room for characters

Jidori allocates a short passage to a specified number of full-width character positions. Instead of occupying only its natural width, the run is composed into a prescribed space—useful for headings, labels, and other brief strings where the underlying character grid matters.

It is not simply the stretching of glyphs. The concern is the space given to the sequence as a whole: expanding gaps when the target is wider, and compressing approximately when it is narrower.

How to use

Select a short Japanese run, choose Apply jidori, and enter the desired number of character cells. Expansion uses character spacing; compression uses scale width in horizontal writing (vertical compression is refused). Remove jidori restores the previous formatting when marinaMoji can identify its own application. The fit is approximate.

Japanese typography

Japanese composition · 禁則処理

Where a line may end

A Japanese line break is not simply a matter of fitting as many characters as possible into the available space. Punctuation belongs visually and grammatically with the characters around it, and a poorly placed break can disturb that relationship.

Kinsoku shori governs characters that should not ordinarily appear at the beginning or end of a line. LibreOffice Writer already implements Japanese line-breaking rules; marinaMoji complements them by exposing relevant composition settings and identifying selected potential issues, without replacing Writer’s layout engine.

How to use

Choose Inspect Japanese Typography to examine the composition settings of the current paragraph or selection. The report also identifies selected potential issues in the text. Inspection is read-only and does not change the document.

Beyond Unicode

Beyond Unicode

When the character you need does not exist

Unicode encodes an enormous repertoire of characters, but not every historical or specialized character form has a suitable representation. When working with manuscripts, older editions, or unusual variants, the exact form a scholar needs may be absent from ordinary text fonts— or may not be encoded at all.

The marinaMoji input method provides tools for composing character forms and inserting them as inline vector graphics. This allows a specialized glyph to appear directly within running text, even when it cannot be entered as an ordinary Unicode character. The LibreOffice typography tools work without the IME; composition and insertion belong to that broader environment.

Chinese characters have always invited ingenious combinations. Three dragons make 龘; three fish make 鱻. But what might two dreams make?

A vector graphic is not a Unicode text character. Searching, copying, accessibility, and interchange may therefore behave differently from ordinary text.
